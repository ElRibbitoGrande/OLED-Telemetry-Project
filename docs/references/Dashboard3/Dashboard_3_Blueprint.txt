The interesting thing is that the visualizer isn't actually that complicated once you decompose it.

I see about six layers:

Particles
    ↑
Vertical light streaks
    ↑
Glowing spectral surface
    ↑
Fine spectral mesh
    ↑
Dark reflective floor
    ↑
Background glow

The "mountains" are almost certainly just an FFT that's been heavily processed.

Instead of drawing this:

||||||||||||||||||||||

it becomes

          /\
        _/  \__
    ___/       \____
 __/                \____

Then:

interpolate between bins
logarithmic frequency scale
smooth over time
add glow
add a reflection
sprinkle animated particles

Suddenly it feels organic.

The DSP side is surprisingly straightforward

Assuming stereo PCM:

PCM Samples
      │
Window Function
      │
2048-point FFT
      │
Magnitude
      │
Log Frequency Mapping
      │
Smoothing
      │
Peak Hold
      │
Renderer

That's basically every music visualizer since Winamp.

The artistry is in the renderer.

I would not render bars

I'd render a continuous mesh.

Think of it as a landscape.

Each x coordinate corresponds to a frequency.

Each y coordinate corresponds to energy.

The renderer connects every point with smooth splines.

That alone gets you 80% of the look.

Then add history

This is what makes it feel alive.

Instead of only drawing

current FFT

store

FFT now
FFT 16 ms ago
FFT 32 ms ago
FFT 48 ms ago
...

Then fade the older ones.

The eye interprets this as motion.

The colors are brilliant

Notice they're not random.

Deep Blue
↓

Cyan
↓

White

↓

Gold

↓

Orange

The palette itself communicates intensity.

No labels needed.

My favorite detail

The vertical light streaks.

Those aren't spectrum bars.

They're decorative.

Every frequency peak emits little vertical rays upward.

They're probably just alpha-blended lines whose brightness follows the FFT.

They're almost free to render.

But they make the whole thing sparkle.

And the particles...

I'd wager they're not tied directly to frequencies.

Just:

Peak occurs

↓

Spawn 8 particles

↓

Float upward

↓

Fade out

Very inexpensive.

Huge perceived complexity.
